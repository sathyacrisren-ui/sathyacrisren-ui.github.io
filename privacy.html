Privacy

Effective 4 October 2026 · Allena

The short version: your training data lives on your phone, and the analysis runs there too. If you sign in, a copy of it is also backed up to our server so a new phone can get it back. This page spells out the rest, plainly.

What stays on your device

Your sessions, sets, weights, reps, effort ratings, runs, rehab check-ins, body scans, bloodwork entries, health metrics read from Apple Health (sleep, heart rate, HRV, steps, nutrition and similar), and every finding the engine produces. All of it is stored locally on your iPhone. The statistics engine that finds patterns in it runs on the phone — this data is not uploaded to analyse it. Signed out, it is never uploaded at all. Signed in, it is backed up (next item) and nothing on the server reads it.

What leaves your device, and why

  • Cloud backup (only if you sign in). When you are signed in, the app sends a copy of your training record to our server after every change, so a new or wiped phone can restore it when you sign in again. The copy is the same bundle you get from Export: sessions, runs, rehab check-ins, body scans, bloodwork, Health numbers and findings. Your sign-in tokens and Strava token are never in it. It is stored as one row that only your account can read or write, encrypted at rest. Nothing on the server opens it, analyses it, or shows it to anyone else; it exists only to be handed back to you. Deleting your account deletes it. Signed out, no backup is made and nothing in your record leaves the phone.
  • Account & race scores (optional). If you sign in to race friends, a small score card syncs to our database: your chosen first name, weekly witnessed-point totals, session counts, a points tier, an activation flag, and a few usage counters (how many deep reads, chats and collection opens, and the date of your last read). The score card has no fields for training content — your sets, weights, exercise names and check-in scores are not on it. Friends and group members can see your score card, counters included; nobody else can, and nobody can see your backup. If a new account joins with a friend's code, the server also records that the code was used and how many free reads each of you has left: a count, nothing about your training.
  • Pods (optional). If you join a pod — three people at a similar stage, sharing one weekly number — two more fields ride that same score card: a stage bracket (roughly how far into rehab you are, or how often you have been training) and a yes/no flag for whether this is an exempt week for you. Those two are health-related, they are the only health-related fields that ever leave your phone, and the screen you opt in on says so before you join. Your actual pain, swelling and check-in numbers never travel — only the yes or no. Leaving a pod is one tap and stops sending them.
  • AI features (optional). If you use the coach chat, the deep read, or photo reading (programs, body-scan sheets, blood results), the relevant text or image is sent to the AI provider (Anthropic) to generate the response, then used for nothing else — it is not used to train models. For the coach chat that context is your training record (sessions with every set, runs, your programme and goals, readiness, the day tags you add (drinks, late caffeine, late meals, stressful days), Apple Health numbers, body scans, blood results, rehab status and check-ins, your notes, and the things you asked Allena to remember, plus the text of a screen when you tap to attach it), so you never have to explain your training to it. A private chat is not saved on your phone and adds nothing to Allena's memory, but it still goes to Anthropic to be answered. Your name and email are never included. The app asks before the first time it sends anything. We also keep a per-day count of how many AI requests your account has made, so the shared quota is fair; that is a number, never your questions. The tree read runs on a schedule (every few days to a week), so we also keep the time of your last one; that is a date, never what it said. Anthropic may keep a request for a limited time under its own retention policy. These features are off until you use them.
  • Strava (optional). If you connect Strava, the access token for your Strava account is stored so imports work. Disconnecting removes it.
  • Nothing else. No analytics SDKs, no advertising identifiers, no location, no contacts, no third-party trackers.

Apple Health

Allena reads Health data (workouts, sleep, heart metrics, nutrition, body mass) only to power your own analysis, on your device. It is never sold, shared, or used for advertising — and Apple's rules forbid that too.

Camera

The knee-measurement tool uses the camera to estimate joint angles live. Nothing is recorded and no images leave the phone. Photo imports (scan sheets, blood results) are read once for the numbers; only a small thumbnail is kept locally if you save it.

Cookies and tracking — this website

This website sets no cookies, runs no analytics, and loads nothing from third parties. That is why there is no cookie banner — there is nothing to consent to.

Backups and deletion

You can export a full backup of your data from Settings at any time — it is a file you own. Deleting the app deletes its local data. If you have an account, Settings › Account has Delete my account: it removes your score card, your cloud backup, friendships, groups and pod membership from the server for good, and signs you out. Your training history stays on the phone.

Not medical advice

Allena tracks and measures; it never diagnoses. Reference ranges shown are the labs' own. Your physio and your doctor are the judges.

Where we operate

Allena is operated from Melbourne, Australia, and handles personal information in line with the Australian Privacy Principles. If you have a privacy question or complaint, email us and we will respond directly.
