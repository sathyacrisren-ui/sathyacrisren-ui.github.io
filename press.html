The one-paragraph pitch

A month before launch, Sathya tore his ACL — and every training app he had quit on him. The running app went silent. The lifting app kept scheduling leg day. So while his knee healed he built Allena: the only coach that counts everything — lifting, running and rehab — against one recovery budget. A hard run makes tomorrow's leg day lighter. A healing knee shapes both. A statistics engine on the phone finds what actually moves the user's numbers and says it in plain sentences, with the evidence attached. The weekly race with friends scores witnessed showing-up only — never output — so an injured athlete can win it.

The story

Allena was built by the athlete it was built for: an ACL-reconstructed lifter-runner working through physio-guided rehab, with no coding background, building daily through recovery. The design refuses the industry's dark patterns by written law — no guilt streaks, no fake urgency, no buyable progress, no AI restating your own screen. Rewards are information: real findings from the user's real data, each showing the days it stands on.

What's genuinely new

  • One recovery budget across three domains — running apps can't feel a squat; lifting apps have never heard of your physio. Allena computes all three together.
  • A camera as a knee protractor — Vision-framework joint measurement in degrees, for physio tracking. Nothing recorded, nothing uploaded.
  • The race money can't win — weekly competition scored on device-witnessed showing-up only. A beginner in knee rehab can beat a bodybuilder.
  • Findings show their evidence — the stats run on-device first; the AI only phrases what survived the maths.
